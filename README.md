# DOT 5m Trading Bot

A personal, single-user research and (eventually) execution project for one
mechanical strategy on `DOT-USDT-SWAP` (OKX perpetual futures), 5-minute
timeframe. It is not a product, not multi-tenant, and not investment advice.
Nothing here recommends that anyone trade this or any other strategy; it is
the author's own tooling for measuring whether a specific, fully-frozen rule
set makes or loses money before any real capital touches it.

The full, authoritative specification (signal rules, trade levels, position
sizing formula, order execution policy, phase plan, execution safety
requirements) lives in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md). This file only summarizes it
and describes the code. If the two ever disagree, CLAUDE.md is correct and
this file is stale.

## Status

- **Phase 1 (Measure): done.** Historical 5m data fetched and validated
  (2020-08-25 to present, zero gaps/duplicates/OHLC violations). Signal
  counts, SL-distance distribution, and one-position-at-a-time survival
  counted.
- **Phase 2 (Backtest): done.** Full P&L with fees, slippage, and funding;
  in-sample/out-of-sample split; intrabar TP/SL ambiguity handled as a
  lower/upper bound. Result: the frozen spec in CLAUDE.md section 4 has
  negative expectancy in every slippage/fee/ambiguity-bound scenario tested,
  in both the in-sample and out-of-sample windows. See
  `data/backtest-report.txt` (generated by `npm run backtest`, not checked
  in) for the full numbers.
- **Phase 2 diagnosis: done.** Follow-up root-cause pass (`npm run
  diagnose`, `npm run charts`) - see "Why Phase 2 is negative" below.
  Short version: the raw signal has a real but razor-thin edge (gross win
  rate ~34-35%, just above the 33.3% zero-cost break-even for a 2:1 R:R) that
  is statistically indistinguishable from a coin flip, and real trading
  costs consistently exceed it. No parameter in CLAUDE.md section 4 was
  changed to reach this conclusion.
- **Phase 3 (Paper trade): not started.** Per CLAUDE.md's phase discipline,
  this does not start until there is a reason to believe Phase 2 would pass
  - it currently does not.
- **Phase 4 (Live) and Phase 5 (Dashboard): not started.**

## Why Phase 2 is negative

A root-cause diagnosis (`npm run diagnose`), run without touching bodyRatio,
RR, the EMA periods, or the strong-candle test, found:

1. **The raw pattern has a thin positive edge, before any cost.** At fee=0 /
   slippage=0 / funding=0, gross win rate is ~34.3% in-sample and ~35.0%
   out-of-sample - just above 1/3, the exact zero-cost break-even for a 2:1
   reward:risk. Gross expectancy is about +0.03R to +0.05R per trade. This
   means the deficit reported in Phase 2 is a **cost problem layered on a
   near-zero signal**, not a total absence of directional information.
2. **That thin edge is not statistically distinguishable from random.** A
   100-trial permutation test replays the exact same entry anchors, the same
   entry/SL/TP construction formula, the same one-position-at-a-time rule,
   the same position sizing, and the same engine (`runScenario`, unmodified)
   - the only thing randomized is which direction (long/short) each anchor
   trades, via a fair coin. The real system's gross win rate lands at the
   **75th percentile** of that random distribution (mean 34.1%, range
   32.4%-36.0%). A real, reliable directional edge would be expected to sit
   much closer to the 95th-99th percentile or above; 75th is consistent with
   "this could easily be luck."
3. **Real costs (taker/maker fees + slippage) consistently exceed the thin
   gross edge**, which is why net expectancy is negative everywhere in the
   Phase 2 report. Round-trip fees alone run roughly 14-22% of R.
4. **The shortfall is broad, not concentrated.** Net expectancy is negative
   across essentially every year (2020-2023, 2026), both sides (long and
   short), all four SL-distance quartiles, and all 24 UTC entry hours - see
   `data/diagnosis-report.txt` for the full breakdown. The one structural
   (not noise) pattern: the tightest-SL quartile loses the most in R terms,
   because fixed per-trade costs eat a bigger fraction of a smaller stop -
   an arithmetic consequence of fee/slippage being roughly fixed while R
   shrinks, not a hidden regime. No filter has been built from any of this.

Manual verification: `data/signal-sample-3days.csv` has every raw signal
(timestamp UTC, side, entry, SL, TP) from 3 randomly chosen days, straight
out of `computeSignal`, for checking against TradingView by hand.

Charts: `data/equity-curve.svg` (log-scale, in-sample/out-of-sample marked),
`data/r-multiple-histogram.svg`, `data/monthly-win-rate.svg` (with the 33.3%
zero-cost and real cost-adjusted break-even lines).

If there's an idea for what to do about this, it belongs in a written
proposal against CLAUDE.md section 4, not a silent parameter change.

## Spec summary

Instrument `DOT-USDT-SWAP`, 5m closed candles only, cross margin, 50x
leverage ceiling (a margin-efficiency ceiling, not the risk control - the
stop-loss is). Signal: three consecutive "strong" candles (body > 50% of
range) in the same direction, with EMA12/26/100 stacked the same direction.
Entry at the signal candle's close; SL at the low/high of the oldest of the
three candles; TP at 2R. One position at a time; a new signal while a
position is open is ignored, never queued. Position size: risk exactly 2% of
current equity, computed from live-fetched `ctVal`/`lotSz`/`minSz`/`tickSz`,
always rounded down. Entry and SL are taker (market); TP is maker (resting
limit). Full detail, rationale, and the execution-safety requirements are in
CLAUDE.md - this paragraph is intentionally incomplete.

  fetchFundingHistory.ts   CLI: fetch and cache available OKX funding-rate-history (limited retention)

  dataIntegrity.ts         validateCandles(), splitIntoContiguousSegments() - used by validateData and the backtest

  signalScan.ts             generateSignals(): slides computeSignal across a contiguous segment
  tradeSequencer.ts         sequenceTrades(): one-position-at-a-time sequencing with real TP/SL touch semantics
                             (used by analyze.ts; backtestEngine.ts has its own equity-aware version, see below)

  instrumentSpec.ts         InstrumentSpec type + loader/validator for the cached instrument spec
  decimal.ts                floorToStep/ceilToStep/roundToStep - decimal-safe rounding to an exchange step size
  positionSizing.ts         computePositionSize(): the frozen 2%-risk sizing formula, pure
  funding.ts                8h funding boundary math (00:00/08:00/16:00 UTC): isFundingTs, countFundingCrossings

  stats.ts                  percentile/mean/sum/monthKey helpers shared by analyze.ts, backtestEngine.ts, diagnose.ts
  format.ts                  pct/usd/num number-formatting helpers shared by backtest.ts and diagnose.ts
  backtestEngine.ts          The Phase 2 engine: signal prep, per-scenario equity-aware simulation

  backtest.ts                CLI: loads data + spec, runs every {slippage x ambiguous-bound x fee-model}

                             count) - a lighter-weight tool than backtest.ts, no fees/sizing/equity.

  randomBaseline.ts          Permutation-test null hypothesis: buildDirectionalVariants() reconstructs
                             both a forced-long and forced-short version of every real signal anchor
                             (same bar2/bar0, same entry/SL/TP formula); runPermutationTrial() feeds a
                             coin-flip-chosen mix through the real runScenario(), unmodified, so "same
                             engine, same structure, only direction randomized" is structurally true,
                             not just asserted. Seeded (mulberry32) for reproducibility.
  diagnose.ts                 CLI: root-cause diagnosis - CSV sample export, gross (pre-cost) expectancy,
                             the permutation test, and year/side/SL-quartile/hour-of-day breakdowns.
                             Writes data/signal-sample-3days.csv, data/diagnosis-report.txt,

  *.test.ts                  node:test unit tests, colocated with the module they cover

### Why `tradeSequencer.ts` and `backtestEngine.ts` both sequence trades

`analyze.ts` (Phase 1) only needs to know which signals survive the
one-position-at-a-time rule and how they'd exit on a touch basis - it has no
concept of equity or position sizing. `backtestEngine.ts` (Phase 2) needs
real P&L, and whether a signal actually opens a position depends on position
sizing, which depends on current equity, which depends on every prior
trade's realized P&L *in that specific scenario*. A signal sizing rejects
(equity too small, or the target risk floors to under `minSz`) never opens a
position and so does not block the next signal - unlike a signal that is
ignored because a real position is still open. That equity-feedback loop is
why `backtestEngine.ts` cannot just reuse `tradeSequencer.ts`'s output as a
fixed sequence and replay fills on top of it; instead it precomputes each
signal's *natural* resolution (scenario-independent - just "where would this
exit if it were the only position") once, then walks the chronological
signal list per scenario, sizing and filling inline. See the comment on
`runScenario` in `backtestEngine.ts` for the full reasoning.

## Data flow

```
OKX public REST API
  |
  |-- GET /market/history-candles  --> fetchData.ts       --> data/DOT-USDT-SWAP-5m.json
  |                                                            data/DOT-USDT-SWAP-1m.json
  |-- GET /public/instruments      --> fetchInstrument.ts --> data/instrument-DOT-USDT-SWAP.json
  |-- GET /public/funding-rate-history --> fetchFundingHistory.ts --> data/funding-rate-history-DOT-USDT-SWAP.json

data/*.json
  |
  |-- validateData.ts   --> console report (integrity only, no trading logic)
  |
  |-- analyze.ts   --> signal.ts + signalScan.ts + tradeSequencer.ts + dataIntegrity.ts
  |                --> console report (Phase 1 measurement)
  |
  |-- backtest.ts  --> instrumentSpec.ts + backtestEngine.ts
  |                    (which itself uses signal.ts, signalScan.ts, positionSizing.ts,
  |                     decimal.ts, funding.ts, stats.ts)
  |                --> console report
  |                --> data/backtest-report.txt   (same report, as text)
  |                --> data/backtest-results.json (full metrics for every scenario)
  |
  |-- diagnose.ts  --> backtestEngine.ts + randomBaseline.ts
  |                --> console report
  |                --> data/signal-sample-3days.csv (manual TradingView cross-check)
  |                --> data/diagnosis-report.txt
  |                --> data/diagnosis-results.json
  |
  \-- charts.ts    --> backtestEngine.ts + svg.ts
                   --> data/equity-curve.svg, data/r-multiple-histogram.svg, data/monthly-win-rate.svg
```

`data/` is gitignored - every file in it is either fetched from OKX or
generated by a script, and is reproduced by rerunning the relevant command.

## Running

All commands run from the repository root, via `npm run <script>`.

```bash
npm run typecheck            # tsc --noEmit
npm test                     # node:test over every src/*.test.ts
```

```bash
npm run fetch-data                        # resume/extend 5m and 1m history backward (default: both bars)
npm run fetch-data -- --bar 5m            # only 5m (also: --bar 1m, --bar both)
npm run fetch-data -- --from 2021-01-01   # anchor a fresh fetch (ignored once data already exists to resume from)
npm run fetch-data -- --to 2021-06-01
npm run fetch-data -- --forward           # catch up to now from whatever is already on disk (cannot combine with --from/--to)
```

```bash
npm run fetch-instrument       # fetch and cache the live instrument spec (run before position sizing/backtest)
npm run fetch-funding-history  # fetch and cache available funding-rate-history (OKX retains only a few months)
```

```bash
npm run validate     # integrity report for data/DOT-USDT-SWAP-{5m,1m}.json
npm run analyze       # Phase 1 measurement report (signal counts, SL-distance distribution, survival count)
npm run backtest       # Phase 2 full backtest: all scenarios, in-sample/out-of-sample, writes data/backtest-*
npm run diagnose        # root-cause diagnosis: CSV sample, gross expectancy, permutation test, breakdowns
npm run charts            # renders the three SVG charts described above into data/
```

`fetch-data`, `fetch-instrument`, and `fetch-funding-history` only hit
public, unauthenticated OKX endpoints - no API keys are needed for anything
in this repository yet (Phase 3+ will need trade-permission keys, per
CLAUDE.md section 6).

## Notes and known limitations

- **Funding cost is a constant-rate approximation.** OKX's
  `funding-rate-history` endpoint only retains a few months (empirically,
  about 3 months for `DOT-USDT-SWAP`), while the backtest covers 2020-08 to
  present. `backtest.ts` measures what fraction of trades hold across a
  funding settlement first; if that is material (>=5%, a threshold set by
  the Phase 2 task instructions, not CLAUDE.md - CLAUDE.md section 4 itself
  just says to model funding unconditionally), it applies the *mean realized
  rate from the available window* as a constant proxy to every crossing
  across the full history, and says so in the report. This is disclosed as
  an approximation, not a real historical cost - there is no way to get the
  real rate for periods OKX no longer retains.
- **1m data is not backfilled to the full history.** It exists to resolve
  "TP and SL both touched in the same 5m bar" (see CLAUDE.md section 5,
  "Intrabar ambiguity"), and per that section is only worth fetching for the
  specific periods containing ambiguous trades if the lower/upper bound
  backtests straddle zero. In the current Phase 2 run they do not (both
  bounds lose money), so 1m data was not needed for this purpose.
- **The permutation test (step 3 of the diagnosis) isolates directional
  skill specifically, not "does trading at these moments at all matter."**
  It reuses the real signals' exact entry timestamps and exact bar2/bar0
  price levels, randomizing only long-vs-short. It does not test whether
  trading at a uniformly random time (instead of at a 3-strong-candle
  moment) would do better or worse - that's a different question this
  diagnosis didn't ask.
- **`data/monthly-win-rate.svg`'s x-axis skips months with zero trades**
  rather than showing them as empty space at their true calendar position.
  Given how few trades exist in 2024-2025 (see the yearly breakdown in
  `data/diagnosis-report.txt`), this compresses what was actually a long,
  nearly-tradeless stretch into a few adjacent bars. Not misleading about
  any individual month's number, just not calendar-accurate spacing.
